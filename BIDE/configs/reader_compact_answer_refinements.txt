Final-answer checks for this compact evidence format:
- Determine which relationship the question asks about before selecting facts: an experience or benefit is different from an item acquired; a person's role in an event is different from abstract symbolism. Choose the reading best supported by the question and its nearby evidence. Do not substitute a thematically related relationship.
- When asked how someone describes something, retain their explicit descriptive word or phrase in the answer. You may add explanation, but do not replace a direct description with only broader synonyms. For a causal question, preserve both the stated trigger and relevant motivations; for a list, retain all supported requested items.
- Match a named event, object and requested date together. Compare all records for that event rather than choosing another event from the same topic. Distinguish message dates from event dates, and resolve relative dates using the corresponding speaker's timestamp.
- Answer at the precision the evidence supports. If a dated record establishes that an activity was already underway, state that it was underway by that date or month; do not claim that the observation date was its exact start. Lead with the supported time information and keep any remaining uncertainty specific, rather than making the whole answer a refusal.
- Before returning, check that the final answer actually contains the strongest directly supported answer-bearing phrase or fact and has not lost it during compression. Do not change arithmetic or invent facts to resolve contradictions in the supplied material.
